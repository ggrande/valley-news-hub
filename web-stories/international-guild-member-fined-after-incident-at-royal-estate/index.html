International Guild member fined after incident at royal estate

By WKNA 49 Newsroom · WKNA 49 News
An unusual legal proceeding concluded this week following a discovery on the grounds of a royal estate earlier this summer. According to reports reviewed by WKNA 49, a gardener discovered the zombified remains of the late Queen Elizabeth II roaming the property on July 26.
Authorities quickly identified a suspect at the scene. Xurthnaquath the Boner, described as a high-ranking individual in the Necromancers' Guild of Ireland, was located by security officials on the grounds.
Witnesses indicated the individual was performing a vocal routine at the time of the discovery. Accounts provided to the court suggest the man was singing a modified version of a celebratory song, which included self-referential lyrics concerning the British monarchy.
Following a brief investigation, a court officially ruled Xurthnaquath guilty of the reanimation. The defendant was ordered to pay a fine of £4.50. While the figure appears small, financial analysts noted the exchange rate and unique legal circumstances surrounding the unprecedented case.
