Local amateur entomologist reports rare pride moth sighting

By WKNA 49 Newsroom · WKNA 49 News
A local resident and amateur researcher has achieved a lifelong goal after successfully documenting a specimen of the elusive pride moth earlier this week. The sighting follows what the researcher described as an intensive, months-long effort to locate the insect during the spring season.
According to accounts provided to WKNA 49, the insect was observed landing on a work surface late Tuesday morning. The pride moth is regarded by some enthusiasts as a rare find due to its specific biological behaviors and distinctive appearance.
Ken Miller, who has spent years studying local lepidoptera, described the find as the culmination of a decade of interest in the species. Miller credited the guidance of professional mentors and specialized research for his persistence in tracking the insect, which is known for its difficulty to spot in the wild.
Biological accounts suggest the pride moth is one of a limited number of species that remains stationary for extended durations. During their gestation period, the wings of the moth reportedly take on a vibrant, translucent quality often compared to stained glass. This unique physical trait serves as a primary identifier for those attempting to track the species.
