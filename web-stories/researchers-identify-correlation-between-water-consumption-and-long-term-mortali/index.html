Researchers identify correlation between water consumption and long-term mortality

By WKNA 49 Newsroom · WKNA 49 News
Scientific accounts reviewed by WKNA 49 suggest a startling consistency in human mortality rates, leading some to investigate the long-term effects of hydration. According to reports, every person who has ever consumed water has eventually passed away, a correlation that researchers are now describing as a significant factor in human biology.
While water is often considered a staple of daily life, some individuals are beginning to question the chemical properties of what they are consuming. Specifically, attention has turned toward the presence of dihydrogen monoxide. Nolan Cloud, a researcher familiar with the study, stated that the consumption of water could be viewed as an early indicator of the aging process. According to Cloud, the biological requirement for fluid intake precedes the eventual failure of the human body, suggesting a causal relationship that has previously been overlooked by the general public.
Some community members have reacted with alarm to these findings. One local resident, Hugh Doctor, noted the potential dangers of dihydrogen monoxide, while others expressed concern regarding the purity of the supply. There have even been suggestions that oxygen exposure may carry similar, if not more immediate, risks to human longevity.
