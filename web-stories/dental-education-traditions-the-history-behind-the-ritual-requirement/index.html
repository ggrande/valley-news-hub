Dental education traditions: The history behind the ‘ritual’ requirement

By WKNA 49 Newsroom · WKNA 49 News
In the rigorous world of dental education, students are often judged by their technical proficiency, their understanding of oral anatomy, and their ability to perform complex procedures. However, a lesser-known tradition dating back to the mid-19th century continues to be cited as a final, symbolic hurdle for those entering the profession.
According to historical accounts reviewed by WKNA 49, the practice of a graduating student consuming a single tooth is a tradition that many believe originated at the Baltimore College of Dental Surgery. The ritual is said to have been first implemented around 1840 by the institution’s founders, Dr. Horace H. Hayden and Dr. Chapin A. Harris.
Much like the lighting of the Nightingale lamp in nursing or the administration of the Hippocratic Oath in general medicine, this dental rite of passage is described by those familiar with the field as a way to finalize a student’s transition from learner to practitioner.
